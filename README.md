# Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style

**Team:** Fourth Dimension\
**Team Members:** Rahul Behra, Kashvi Patil, Soumili Basu

## Overview

This project is based on the research paper *"Can You Make It Sound Like
You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style"* by Connor
Baumler, Calvin Bao, Huy Nghiem, Xinchen Yang, Marine Carpuat, and Hal
Daumé III (University of Maryland).

The paper investigates whether people can edit text generated by a large
language model (LLM) so that it better reflects their own writing style,
especially in personal writing tasks such as wedding vows, apology
letters, and condolence messages.

## Problem Statement

LLM-generated writing can be fluent and useful, but it may sound generic
or fail to reflect an individual writer's voice. The study examines
whether human post-editing can make AI-generated drafts feel more
personal and stylistically authentic.

## Objectives

-   Study how post-editing changes the stylistic similarity of
    AI-generated writing to a person's unassisted writing.
-   Examine whether post-edited text retains detectable stylistic
    characteristics associated with LLM output.
-   Compare automatic style measurements with participants' perceptions
    of authenticity.
-   Understand participants' willingness to use post-editing for
    personal writing.

## Study Methodology

The paper reports a pre-registered online study involving **81
participants** in the final analysis.

Participants completed writing tasks under two conditions:

1.  **Control condition:** Participants wrote text themselves using
    their planned details.
2.  **Treatment condition:** Participants received an LLM-generated
    draft based on their planned details and then edited the draft to
    better match their personal style.

The researchers measured stylistic similarity using **LUAR text
embeddings** and also examined AI-detector scores from **PANGRAM**.
Participants reported how authentic and usable they found their writing.

## Key Findings

-   Post-editing made the resulting text stylistically more similar to
    participants' own unassisted writing.
-   Post-editing reduced stylistic similarity to the original
    LLM-generated text.
-   Even after editing, the text remained stylistically closer to
    LLM-generated writing than to participants' fully human-written
    control text.
-   Post-edited writing was more stylistically diverse than raw LLM
    output, but less diverse than unassisted human writing.
-   Participants often felt that their edited text represented their
    personal style, even when automatic measurements detected remaining
    LLM-style patterns.
-   A majority of participants preferred the post-editing workflow for
    future personal writing tasks. The paper reports that 58
    participants preferred post-editing, 20 preferred writing without
    AI, and 3 preferred using model-generated text without editing.
-   Participants identified the lack of originality in AI-generated
    drafts as an important obstacle to adoption.

## Conclusion

The study suggests that people can meaningfully personalize
LLM-generated drafts through post-editing, but editing does not
necessarily remove all stylistic traces of AI generation. It also
highlights a difference between automatic style measurements and
people's own judgments of whether a text sounds like them.

## Limitations

The paper notes several limitations:

-   Style was measured primarily with computational models, which may
    not capture every aspect of personal style.
-   Participants used simulated writing scenarios and were instructed
    not to include real identifying details.
-   The study focused on particular writing tasks and one post-editing
    workflow.
-   The study did not directly evaluate how the eventual recipients of
    the writing would perceive the edited text.
